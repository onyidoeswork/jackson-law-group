Delivery Vehicle Accident Lawyers in New York City

Get Your Free Case EvaluationCall (212) JACKSON

Package vans, box trucks, and food delivery riders are on every block, on tight schedules, often double parked or cutting through bike lanes. The pressure to deliver fast causes crashes.

The first question in these cases is who the driver was really working for.

Vehicles Involved

  • Package delivery vans and box trucks
  • Restaurant and app-based food delivery cars, mopeds, and e-bikes
  • Grocery and furniture delivery trucks
  • Couriers and messenger services

Who May Be Responsible

A company is generally responsible for the carelessness of an employee who is on the job. Large delivery companies often use contractors and then argue the driver was not their employee. We look past the label to who controlled the work, the route, the schedule, and the vehicle.

  • The driver
  • The delivery company or its local contractor
  • The owner of the vehicle, who may be responsible for a driver using it with permission. Our office will confirm what applies to your claim after we discuss it and complete the necessary research.
  • A restaurant or store that employs its own delivery workers

Evidence We Go After

  • The delivery app and route data for that trip
  • In-vehicle cameras and telematics
  • The contract between the company and its delivery contractor
  • Driver hiring, training, and schedule records
  • Street and building camera video

What You Can Recover

  • Medical bills, past and future
  • Lost wages and loss of future earning ability
  • Pain and suffering
  • Loss of enjoyment of life

To recover for pain and suffering after a motor vehicle crash, New York law requires a "serious injury." Fractures, significant scarring, and significant or permanent loss of use of a body part are examples.

Deadlines

No-fault benefits pay medical bills and part of lost wages, up to $50,000 per person, no matter who caused the crash. Written notice to the insurer is generally due within 30 days of the accident.

Most New York injury lawsuits must be filed within three years of the accident.

A Postal Service vehicle is different. Claims against the federal government follow federal rules with their own deadlines. Every case is different, and the rules that apply depend on the facts, the parties, and the insurance involved. Once we discuss your claim and complete the necessary research, our office will confirm which deadlines and coverage apply to you.

Common Questions

The driver said he is an independent contractor. Does that protect the company?

Not automatically. Courts look at how much control the company had over the work. There may also be insurance the company requires its contractors to carry.

A delivery rider on an e-bike hit me on the sidewalk. Do I have a case?

You may, against the rider and possibly the business the rider was working for. Insurance coverage for e-bikes varies, so we investigate it early.

The van was double parked and I crashed into traffic going around it. Is the company responsible?

It can be. Illegal parking that forces others into danger can be a cause of a crash. The facts and any video will matter.
